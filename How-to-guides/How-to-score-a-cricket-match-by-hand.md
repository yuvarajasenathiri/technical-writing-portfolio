# Score a cricket match by hand

Use a standard linear scorebook to record and verify the events of a cricket match.

This guide explains how to set up a scorebook, record deliveries and dismissals, complete an innings, and verify that the scorebook balances.

> **Note:** Scorebook notation varies by scorebook format, region, and scoring convention. Before play begins, agree on the notation with the other scorer, and record any variations in the scorebook.

> **Note:** These instructions assume a match with six-ball overs, two scorers, and a linear scorebook.

## Before you begin

Make sure that you have the following items:

- A linear scorebook
- Two sharpened pencils and an eraser
- A team sheet from each captain
- A copy of the playing conditions
- A watch or clock

Before play begins:

- Sit where you can clearly see the umpire at the bowler's end.
- Sit next to the other scorer, if possible.
- Review the [umpire signals](#umpire-signals).

> **Important:** Use a pencil to score a match. You might need to correct entries during play.

## Scorebook layout

A linear scorebook contains the following sections:

| Section | Description |
|---|---|
| Batting section | Records deliveries faced, dismissals, and runs scored by each batter. |
| Bowling section | Records deliveries, overs, and bowling figures for each bowler. |
| Extras section | Records byes, leg byes, wides, no-balls, and penalty runs. |
| Summary section | Records the running total, fall of wickets, and final innings figures. |

## Set up the scorebook

1. Record the team names, venue, date, and match format.
2. Record the toss result and the captain's decision.
3. In the batting section, enter the batters in the order that the team sheet lists them.
4. In the bowling section, enter the expected bowlers.
5. Record the scheduled number of overs and the maximum number of overs for each bowler.
6. Record the start time.
7. Record any scoring notation that you agreed on with the other scorer.

## Record a delivery

Record each delivery before the bowler bowls the next ball.

1. Wait for the ball to become dead.
2. Check the umpire's signal.
3. Acknowledge the signal to the umpire.
4. In the bowling section, record the outcome in the next box.
5. In the batting section, record the same outcome for the striker if the striker scores runs off the bat or doesn't score. For an extra, follow the steps in [Record an extra](#record-an-extra).
6. If the striker hits a four or six, circle the numeral.
7. Update the running total.
8. Confirm that the batters change ends when they complete an odd number of runs and at the end of the over.

### Delivery notation

| Outcome | Mark |
|---|---|
| No run | A dot |
| Runs scored | The numeral, such as 1, 2, or 3 |
| Four or six | The numeral inside a circle |
| Wide | A cross, with one additional stroke on an arm for each extra run |
| No-ball | A circle with the letters NB written beside it, and any runs scored written inside the circle |
| Bye | A triangle, with the number of runs inside |
| Leg bye | An inverted triangle, with the number of runs inside |
| Wicket | The letter W |

> **Note:** A boundary and a no-ball both use a circle. The letters NB beside a no-ball circle distinguish it from a circled four or six.

## Record an extra

An extra adds runs to the team total without crediting the runs to a batter.

1. Identify the extra from the umpire's signal.
2. In the bowling section, record the extra in the next box.
3. In the extras section, record the runs in the appropriate column.
4. If the batter scores runs off the bat from a no-ball, credit those runs to the batter. The penalty run for the no-ball remains an extra.
5. Charge wides and no-balls to the bowler.
6. For a wide or no-ball, record an additional delivery. A wide or no-ball isn't a legal delivery, so the bowler must bowl another ball.
7. Update the running total.

> **Important:** Don't charge byes or leg byes to the bowler. Record penalty runs in the extras section, and don't charge them to the bowler.

## Record a dismissal

1. In the bowling section, record W for the delivery.
2. In the row for the batter, record the dismissal method by using the notation in the following table.
3. Record the fielder who takes the catch or who effects the run out, if applicable.
4. Record the bowler who takes the wicket, if applicable.
5. Record the batter's total runs.
6. Record the number of balls that the batter faced.
7. In the fall of wickets section, record the wicket number and team score. For example, 3-45 means that the third wicket fell at a team score of 45.
8. In the next row, record the incoming batter.

### Dismissal notation

| Dismissal | Notation in the batter's row |
|---|---|
| Bowled | b, followed by the bowler's name |
| Caught | c, followed by the fielder's name, and then b and the bowler's name |
| Leg before wicket (LBW) | lbw b, followed by the bowler's name |
| Run out | run out, followed by the fielder's name in parentheses |
| Stumped | st, followed by the wicketkeeper's name, and then b and the bowler's name |

> **Note:** Don't credit a bowler for a run out, obstruction, or timed out dismissal.

## Complete an over

1. Confirm that the over contains six legal deliveries.
2. Calculate the runs that the bowler conceded.
3. Update the bowler's analysis.
4. If the bowler conceded no runs, mark the over as a maiden with the letter M.
5. Update the bowler's cumulative overs, maidens, runs, and wickets.
6. Verify that the running total matches the sum of the batting and extras sections.
7. Compare the score, wickets, and overs with the other scorer.

> **Note:** An over is still a maiden if the only runs in it were byes or leg byes.

> **Tip:** Reconcile scores at the end of every over to identify errors early.

## Close an innings

1. Draw a line below the final batting entry.
2. Record each batter who remains not out.
3. Total each extras column.
4. Record the final score, wickets, and overs.
5. Complete the final figures for each bowler.
6. Record the finish time and innings duration.
7. Verify the scorebook.

## Verify the scorebook

Complete this procedure before the next innings begins.

1. Total the runs scored by all batters.
2. Total all extras.
3. Confirm that batting runs plus extras equal the team total.
4. Total the runs that all bowlers conceded.
5. Add byes, leg byes, and penalty runs to the bowling total.
6. Confirm that the result equals the team total.
7. Confirm that the total overs bowled equal the total overs for the innings.
8. Confirm that the total dismissals equal the wickets that fell.
9. Compare the scorebook with the other scorer.

The scorebook balances when:

- Batting runs plus extras equal the team total.
- Runs conceded by bowlers plus byes, leg byes, and penalty runs equal the team total.
- Overs bowled equal the total overs in the innings.
- Dismissals equal the wickets that fell.

## Troubleshoot balance check issues

| Issue | Possible cause | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| The bowling total is lower than the team total by the number of byes, leg byes, and penalty runs. | Byes, leg byes, or penalty runs were omitted during reconciliation. | Include byes, leg byes, and penalty runs in the calculation. |
| The bowling total exceeds the team total. | A bye or leg bye was charged to a bowler. | Remove the runs from the bowler's analysis. |
| An over contains seven or more deliveries. | A wide or no-ball was counted as a legal delivery. | Review the over, and correct the delivery count. |
| The wickets credited to bowlers exceed the number of dismissals. | A run out was credited to a bowler. | Remove the bowler credit. |
| The batting total exceeds the team total by four or six runs. | A boundary was recorded twice. | Remove the duplicate entry. |

## Umpire signals

| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Both arms extended horizontally | Wide |
| One arm extended at shoulder height | No-ball |
| One arm raised with an open palm | Bye |
| Tap on the raised knee | Leg bye |
| Arm waved across the chest | Boundary four |
| Both arms raised above the head | Six |
| Index finger raised | Out |
| Wrists crossed below the waist | Dead ball |
| Hand touching the opposite shoulder | Penalty runs to the batting side |
